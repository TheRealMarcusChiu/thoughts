# Justice 

- justice is love that upholds righteousness
- mercy is love that withholds deserved punishment
- forgiveness is love that cancels personal debt
- grace is love that lavishes undeserved favor

# The Gospel Brings Them All Together

- justice - sin is truly punished
- mercy - believers are spared the punishment they deserve
- forgiveness - god no longer counts their sins against them
- grace - god adopts them as His children, gives them eternal life, and makes them heirs with Christ

The gospel is not GOD choosing love instead of justice. Rather, it is LOVE satisfying JUSTICE so that MERCY, FORGIVENESS, and GRACE can be given without compromising RIGHTEOUSNESS.

# The judge enters the courtroom as the accused

This is the surprising part.

Imagine a judge who has the authority to sentence a criminal. But then, out of love, the judge steps down from the bench, takes the criminal's place, and accepts the sentence himself.

In ordinary human justice, this would be impossible because the judge and criminal are separate people.

But Christianity claims something unique:

- Humanity is guilty before God's holiness.
- God Himself enters humanity in Christ.
- Christ bears the consequence of sin.

"The justice system is itself a witness to the gospel message"

"In the same way God created Eve for Adam because no animal was a suitable helper, God became man as Christ Jesus because no animal was a suitable sacrifice"

Jesus does not merely die "instead of us"; He dies "as one of us"


# The Old Testament points toward this pattern

The sacrificial system in Israel hints at this idea.

An innocent animal would bear the symbolic burden of sin:

"The life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it for you on the altar to make atonement for your souls."
— Leviticus 17:11

But those sacrifices were incomplete because animals could not truly represent humanity.

Christ fulfills this pattern:

"Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!"
— John 1:29

He is both:
- the Lamb being offered
- the Lord who provides the sacrifice
