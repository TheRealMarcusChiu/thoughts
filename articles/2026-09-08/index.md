We often talk about love as giving.

And yes - love gives.

But before any of us gave, we received.

We received breath.

We received a body.

We received a name before we could pronounce it.

We received food before we could earn it.

We received language from mouths that spoke to us before we could understood it.

Paul asks, "What do you have that you did not receive?"

That question reaches all the way down.

To love, is to also be a receiver.

And that is not humiliation.

That is grace.

Peter once tried to refuse this. When Jesus knelt to wash his feet, Peter protested. Perhaps receiving felt too exposed. Perhaps giving would have felt more noble. But Jesus told him that unless he allowed himself to be washed, he could not share in His life.

Sometimes giving is easier than receiving.

Giving can let me feel strong.

Giving can let me control the terms.

Giving can even become a way of making sure I never have to say, "I need you"

But Jesus wraps a towel around His waist, kneels before Peter, and says, in effect:

"Let Me love you in the place where you cannot clean yourself"

Healthy love has a rhythm.

A gift is freely given.

It is gratefully received.

And then it returns - not as payment, not as debt, but as another gift.

Reciprocity does not always mean fifty-fifty, every day, at every moment.

An infant receives almost everything. A grieving friend may have no strength to return our care. A disabled member of the body of Christ does not have to prove equal productivity in order to belong. A caregiver may need the whole community to hold up the arms that have grown tired.

Love is not a ledger.

Sometimes reciprocity appears across years, not minutes.

Sometimes the gift a child gives a parent is not repayment, but trust.

Sometimes the gift a suffering friend gives is simply the courage to let us stay.

And sometimes the holiest sentence in a relationship is not "I can handle it."

It is "Please help me."
